USUS market

  • Fri close: Nasdaq Composite +1.09% to 24,579.42, S&P 500 +0.31% to 7,376.86, Dow -0.46% to 51,285.73; SOX +4.21% to 9,390.25 after Thursday's about -12% wipeout, and VIX fell 9.01% to 25.64 (CNBC, Yonhap Infomax).
  • On the week the Nasdaq lost 3.4%, the S&P 2.7% (worst week since June) and the Dow 0.9%; XLK fell about 8%, its worst week since early April 2025, and S&P energy was near -12% as Brent slid about 16% to $98.32 and WTI closed at $90.83 (CNBC, Yonhap Infomax).
  • October payrolls rose 165,000 (Dow Jones via CNBC had expected about 59,000; other wires cited about 65,000), unemployment slipped to 4.1%, the prior two months were revised up by a combined 84,000, and average hourly earnings rose 0.4% m/m; the 10-year yield was about 4.86%, the 2-year about 4.52%, and the dollar index jumped to 102.28 (CNBC, Yonhap Infomax).
  • Kalshi prices a 77% chance of a 25bp hike on Dec 9 (hold 23%), up from 65% on Nov 5 after the jobs print; next week brings October CPI on Tuesday, CoreWeave results the same day, Cisco and Applied Materials on Thursday, PPI on Friday, and a closed Treasury market on Veterans Day Wednesday (Kalshi via OddsOnAir, Yardeni).

EuropeEurope market

  • Fri close: STOXX 600 -1.00% to 648.33, DAX -1.92% to 25,820.36, FTSE 100 -1.01% to 10,673.94 and CAC 40 -0.81% to 8,139.00, off session lows after Wall Street's chip bounce (MarketWatch, Sharecast).
  • Teleperformance sank about 15% after a weaker revenue outlook and the AI backlash; BE Semiconductor, ASML (+2.69%) and Aixtron finished higher with the US semiconductor rebound (Sharecast).
  • Goldman's public Market Pulse (Nov 5) still expects the ECB to deliver one more hike in December to a 2.75% peak; no fresh European session until Monday (GSAM Market Pulse).

HKHong Kong

  • Hang Seng -1.27% to 24,481.86 on HK$247.43bn of turnover, but +5.1% on the week, its best since March 2025; Hang Seng Tech -1.83% to 4,078.23 (+8.6% on the week) and the China Enterprises Index -1.1% to 8,274 (RTHK).
  • Shanghai Composite -1.1% to 3,649 yet +1.4% on the week, snapping a six-week losing streak; CSI 300 -1.4% and +1.7% weekly, ending a 12-week slide, as bargain hunting followed Beijing support talk (RTHK).
  • US-facing optical names were hit hard, with Zhongji Innolight -9.2% and Eoptolink -7.3%; Bloomberg says the AI unwind is pushing some investors back toward out-of-favor Chinese and Indian stocks (RTHK, Bloomberg).

TokyoTokyo

  • Nikkei 225 -5.96% to 57,434.77, more than 20% below its June peak (a bear market) and -9.3% on the week, the worst weekly drop in more than six years; Topix -5.9% with all 33 industry groups lower, yen near 158.5 per dollar (RTHK, CNBC).
  • Kospi -6.02% to about 5,059 (after an early drop of as much as 8% that tripped a breaker) and -11.7% on the week; it is about 44% below its June peak but still up about 20% this year (RTHK).
  • Taiwan's TAIEX -4.51% to 39,701.64, also into a bear market, after an intraday plunge of 3,737.67 points; TSMC about -9.6%. From Monday the FSC will cut the daily stock-loan short cap from 30% to 3% of 30-day average volume, raise minimum short-sale margin from 90% to 130%, widen collateral that can cover margin calls, and encourage buybacks; the National Security Fund started its 10th market-support operation (CNA, CNBC).

L/SLong/short & hedge fund flows

  • ORTEX estimates about $57.8bn of paper gains for US shorts on the week, with Thursday alone about $97.6bn as AI and chips broke; Nvidia shorts made an estimated $5.5bn, Micron $4.5bn and Tesla $3.0bn (ORTEX).
  • Memory, storage and optical names fell 22% to 36% on the week (Lumentum -36%, Seagate -29%, Marvell and Western Digital -28%, Sandisk -27%, Micron -22%); shorts in those six made an estimated $13.4bn, about a third of it in Micron (ORTEX).
  • Weekly recap of Goldman's October prime book (via Hedgeweek, Nov 4): net leverage fell 4.2 percentage points to 73.4%, the steepest monthly drop since March; fundamental L/S -3.5% and systematic -3.6%, the first month since 2016 that both lost more than 3%, with about $20bn of tech sold over two weeks (Hedgeweek).
  • Activ8Insights logged a Bear Cave short report on Twist Bioscience (TWST) dated Nov 5; no new Muddy Waters report since EIX on Oct 13 (Activ8Insights).

US

  • No new SEC press release since the Nov 4 small-entity definition update; no new CFTC release since the Nov 5 impartial-access interpretation already covered Friday (SEC, CFTC).
  • The jobs surprise put a December hike back in play: Kalshi 77% for 25bp on Dec 9, and Goldman's Market Pulse (Nov 5) still expects a December hike, with March data-dependent (Kalshi via OddsOnAir, GSAM).

Europe

  • Sweden's FI fined Apicem AS SEK 850,000 for late insider-transaction filings in H100 Group AB, and director-general Johan Almenberg told the Bank Association on Nov 5 to judge new technology, including crypto, by the customer need it serves rather than the technology itself (FI).
  • No fresh ESMA press release in the window; GSAM's Market Pulse still sees one more ECB hike in December to 2.75% (ESMA, GSAM).

Asia

  • Taiwan's FSC will apply five market-stability measures from Nov 9, including a cut in the daily stock-loan short cap from 30% to 3% of 30-day average volume (with hedging exceptions) and a rise in minimum short-sale margin from 90% to 130% (CNA).
  • Hong Kong's SFC published consultation conclusions on barring unregulated firms from misleading titles and will take an amendment bill covering the SFO and an AMLO provision for virtual-asset trading platforms to LegCo (SFC 26PR187).
  • Japan's FSA hosted the seventh Japan-EU Joint Financial Regulatory Forum in Tokyo on Nov 4-5, covering market risks, digital finance, AI and capital markets; the next meeting is due in Brussels in 2027 (Japan FSA).
